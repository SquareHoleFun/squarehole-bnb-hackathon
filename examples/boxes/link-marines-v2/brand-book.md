# Link Marines, version 2: brand book

## Identity

**Name:** Link Marines, version 2. The box's name stays Link Marines, the name counsel cleared. **Ticker:** MARINE,
the founder's choice. **Price coin:** LINK. **In the box:** 420 marines in a new collection, twelve ranks from
Private to General.

**One-sentence joke:** At mess night the General gets exactly the same ration as the Private.

**Cover line:** "The same ration for all 420."

**The literal line next to it:** "In plain words, ten of every fifteen fee coins are split into 420 equal shares, one for each marine, whatever its rank."

**Motto:** "Not selling."

**The literal line next to it:** "In plain words, a marine keeps its rations until its owner claims them. Nobody else can take them."

**Story hook:** Every buy and every sale pays a small fee in LINK. Ten of its fifteen parts are the marines'
rations, split into 420 equal shares, one for each marine. A marine that someone owns keeps its ration on its plate
until its owner claims it in LINK. A marine still in the box sends its ration into the box's own pool, so the pool
gets deeper while the marines wait. Rank is only the picture. The Private and the General get the same ration.

"Link Marines" is the nickname a coin's community uses for its keenest fans, and the community has long joked about
ranks from Private to General. This box takes the joke to a formal dinner. The squad sits at mess night in dress navy
and white caps, and every plate holds the same ration. The joke is on the marines and their table manners. It is
never on real service members, on the coin's community or on the people who buy, sell or hold.

Version 1 of this kit is the field mess hall, where the marines eat the fees. This version is mess night, where the
marines get them as rations. The two kits share the name and nothing else.

## The ticker

MARINE is the ticker because the founder chose it. A token on Solana has used the same ticker since 13 September
2026, so every live page and every post shows the chain and the full contract address. Public texts never name that
token and never attack it.

## What this box does

Six things the box does by its rules, said plainly. They describe the box. They are no promise about value.

| What | In plain words |
| --- | --- |
| Real LINK | The box is meant for Ethereum and priced in LINK itself. Fees, rations and the pool are all LINK. |
| 420 marines that carry their rations | Each marine's share waits on that marine until its owner claims it, and goes with the marine when it changes hands. |
| Claims nobody can stop | Only the marine's owner can claim. There is no minimum, no tip and no time limit, and nobody can pause a claim. |
| Rules nobody can change | The box has no owner or admin with any power. Its settings are fixed when it is created, for Square Hole too. |
| A deeper pool while marines wait | The share of a marine still in the box goes into the box's own pool. This uses the Depth policy from step 5 of v2.1. |
| A box that fails safely | If the raise misses its 90 days, the box fails, the waiting rations join the closing pool and holders claim their part. |

## Voice and tone

We sound like the mess sergeant at a formal dinner, reading out the menu: short, warm, a little proud that everyone
gets the same plate. Pictures and numbers come first, words second. We say what happens in the order it happens.
When something can go wrong, we say it plainly and leave the joke out.

- Use the plain words: box, fee, coin, ration, claim, pool, holder, marine, rank. Put the literal line next to every
  joke.
- A ration is a marine's share of the fees, in LINK. Its owner claims it. Never call it pay, and never say a marine
  or anyone else is paid. The avoid list below has the words we leave out.
- Say "the box token MARINE" and "a marine", and keep the two apart. MARINE in capitals is the box's token, and a
  marine is one of the 420 NFTs. Holding MARINE gives no ration and is not owning a marine.
- No urgency, no countdowns, no prices, and no talk about what MARINE, LINK or a marine might be worth.
- In pictures the price coin is a plain gold coin with no name. In text, name it LINK where a sentence needs a name.
  Never name the project or company behind it.
- Art lettering is in stencil capitals. Everywhere else we write in sentence case.

In the story, gold coins stand for the price coin, and a white plate with one gold coin on it is one marine's ration.

plain symbols. The coins carry no letters, no symbol and no hexagon. When a page shows real balances, it also writes

## Typography

Live text uses three free font families under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, all on Google Fonts, the same three as
version 1.

| Use | Family and weights | Fallback stack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Titles | Saira Stencil One, 400 | `"Saira Stencil One", "Arial Black", Impact, system-ui, sans-serif` |
| Body and buttons | Barlow, 400 to 700 | `"Barlow", system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", Roboto, sans-serif` |
| Numbers, counts, ids | Courier Prime, 400 and 700 | `"Courier Prime", ui-monospace, "SF Mono", Menlo, Consolas, monospace` |

Saira Stencil One sits well next to the stencil letters in the art. Barlow is plain and easy to read at small sizes.
Courier Prime looks like a typed menu card, and its numbers are all the same width, so amounts, counts and marine ids
line up.

The pictures need no font at all. Every letter in the art is drawn from the kit's own stencil alphabet, described in
[art-notes.md](art-notes.md). This kit ships no font files. A site that uses these fonts self-hosts pinned files with
their license notice. Body text is at least 16 px with a line height of 1.5.

## Logo

The mark is a round khaki patch with a stitched edge. On it, the Private grins under a white peaked cap with a plain
gold coin as its badge, in a dress-navy jacket with a bow tie. The patch sits on the walnut panelled wall. The files
are `art/logo.svg` and `art/logo.png` (1024), `art/x-avatar.png` (400) and `art/logo-48.png`, which shows the mark
still reads at 48 px.

- **Construction.** One module is 1/32 of the square, 32 px on the 1024 px logo. The patch is a circle of 13 modules
  radius in the middle of the square, with an outline of 0.62 modules. Its stitches run 1.1 modules inside the rim.
  The marine is the collection's first rank, the Private, scaled to 0.86 of the bust box, with the cap's top about
  4.5 modules down, cut off by the patch's circle at the chest.
- **Why the Private.** Every marine gets the same ration, and the Private is the first rank on the community's
  ladder. The General stays off the mark, so the mark never points at any one person.
- **Clear space.** Keep at least 2 modules free on every side of the patch. The square files already include it, and
  `check.mjs` proves that the border holds only bare wall.
- **Round crop.** In a round avatar, the whole patch sits inside 95% of the circle. `check.mjs` proves this too.
- **Smallest size.** 48 px. Below that, write MARINE in live text instead.
- **Keep** the khaki patch, its stitches, the white cap, its gold coin and the navy jacket together.
- **Don't** recolor the patch, the cap or the jacket, add words or the ticker to the patch, swap the coin for a coin
  with a symbol, stretch, rotate or outline the mark, or set it on a light ground without its walnut square.

On the X banner, the bottom-left corner (x 0 to 420, y 270 to 500) holds only bare wall, so the avatar can overlap it.
`check.mjs` renders the banner and its bare wall and compares that corner pixel by pixel. Keep words at least 60 px
from every edge of the banner and 48 px from the edges of every other piece. The wide box banner can be cropped on
narrow screens, so the page also shows the name in live text.

## Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Draw marines as cartoon busts in white peaked caps and plain dress-navy mess jackets | Copy a real service's emblem, insignia, rank badges, chevrons, stripes, flags, unit patches or camouflage |
| Show rank with the place card and the picture's name | Show rank with coins, stripes or stars, or give a higher rank more of anything |
| Give every marine the same plate with one gold coin | Give the General a bigger plate, more coins or a special wall |
| Give the marines cutlery, a cup, a jug or a menu card | Draw weapons, fights, food piled high or anyone getting hurt |
| Show the price coin as a plain gold coin | Draw the price coin's logo, its blue, a hexagon, a cube, chains or a coin with a symbol |
| Keep blue for cloth: jackets, cap bands, sleeves and the cook's one flannel shirt | Paint a wall, a ground or a word in navy or slate |
| Show the token MARINE as an orange dog tag with a hole and three stamped lines | Write the ticker or a number on the dog tags |
| Draw every letter from the kit's stencil alphabet | Use a font in a picture |
| Put each fee picture next to its caption | Show price charts, rockets or anything rising in value |
| Keep the joke on the marines and their table manners | Make fun of real service members, the coin's community or the people behind the coin |

ground for text and never text. `check.mjs` recomputes every ratio in this table.

- Every SVG carries a title and a description. The storybook uses the same words as alt text.
- Repeat any words from a banner or the cover as live text on the page.
- Never show owned, waiting or in the box by color alone. Say it in words.

- Amounts, marine ids, rations and the contract address are selectable text, never only pictures. Controls are at
  least 44 by 44 px with a visible focus ring, black on light grounds and white on walnut.

## Homage and IP

- The name borrows a community's nickname for its keenest fans, and the ranks borrow its old joke ladder from
  Private to General. The homage is in the idea only. The founder reports that counsel cleared the name, and the
  counsel pack records that clearance as the name only.
- The project and company behind LINK are never named. Their logo, their blue, their hexagon and their product words
  never appear, and the art has no chains. LINK is named in plain text only, as the price coin, and every public page
  carries the "not affiliated" line from the voice table.
- The marines are caricatures of nobody. The caps and jackets are generic shapes with no emblem, insignia, rank
  badge, chevron, stripe, flag, unit patch, motto or camouflage of any real service. The cap badge is the kit's own
  plain gold coin. No marine is drawn after a real person, and none holds a weapon.
- Dress navy with white caps is a look many services share. Next to the word marines it points toward one of them,
  so the kit keeps every other part generic and asks counsel to look at the cover and one marine first.
- Every face is the same ration-paper color, the unprinted paper of a screen print, so no marine stands for a real
  group of people. Braids, mustaches, spectacles, freckles and props tell the squad apart.
- One flannel shirt appears, on the cook in one story scene, as a nod to a community in-joke. The cook has two
  braids and freckles and looks like nobody real. Seven candles stand on the box banner's table, the kit's one quiet
  seven. Never three sevens in a row.
- No known meme characters, mascots or characters appear. The squad is drawn for this kit.
- All art here is original and drawn in code for this kit. Nothing is traced, downloaded or generated from other
  images, and the pictures use no font.
- Square Hole is named as the platform. This kit names no other Square Hole token or collection.
- The ticker MARINE is the founder's choice. "The ticker" above says what every live page and post shows next to it.
- Counsel still has to look at the joke, the look, the "not affiliated" line, the ticker and the license for
  publishing this kit. See [details.md](details.md).
