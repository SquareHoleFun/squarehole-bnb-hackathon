# One Dollar is all you need brand book

## Identity

**Name:** One Dollar is all you need. **Ticker:** 1. **Price coin:** USDC.
**In the box:** exactly one USDC, the dollar.

**One-sentence joke:** The token is 1, and the dollar is in the box.

**Story hook:** Every trade pays a small fee in USDC. On buys and on sales alike, ten of its 15 parts buy the token and burn
it. No fee goes into the coin pile, so fees never add to the dollar inside. After graduation, anyone can burn the token to
take its slice of the dollar.

Many tokens come with a big story about what stands behind them. This box shows everything it holds. It is one
dollar, framed under glass on a gallery wall, in a spotlight, behind a velvet rope. A shop frames its first dollar the
same way: on show, and never spent. The dollar is the star of the show, and it does nothing at all. That is the joke,
and it is also the rule. The dollar is never a buyer, a seller, a holder or the creator.

The meme is in the name. One Dollar is all you need, and the box holds exactly that: one dollar. The token's ticker is
1. That makes the second joke. The token is 1. It is not a dollar, it never equals one and it never follows one.
The dollar is in the box.

In the pictures the dollar is a cartoon one-dollar bill, drawn for this kit. It stands for the one USDC in the coin
pile. There are a billion tokens at the start, so each token's slice is a billionth of the dollar. The pictures show it
plainly. A heap of rose tokens towers next to one framed bill.

**Where the idea comes from.** The founder's idea is a satire box that holds exactly one dollar of a stable coin as
its coin pile. For version 2 the founder chose the name One Dollar is all you need and the ticker 1, and asked for a dollar
note rather than a coin in the pictures. On 2026-10-23 he set the rule that the kit never writes the ticker with a
dollar sign in front: the ticker is 1. See [SH-KIT-ONE-DOLLAR.md](SH-KIT-ONE-DOLLAR.md).
<!-- founder-words:end -->

**Cover line:** "The token is 1. The dollar is in the box."

Wherever the cover line appears, the literal line goes next to it. The joke is the wordplay, and the literal line is
the rule. It reads "In plain words, the token is called 1 and the box holds one USDC. On buys and on sales, ten of every 15 fee parts buy the token and burn it."

The ticker comes with trade-offs, such as a cashtag on X that does not work and a bare 1 that can read as a number.
[details.md](details.md) writes them down.

## Voice and tone

We sound like a museum guide who takes one dollar very seriously. Quiet, exact and polite, a little proud of the
dollar. The joke is never explained. Pictures and numbers come first, words second. We say what happens in the order
it happens. When something can go wrong, we say it plainly and leave the joke out.

- Use the plain words: fee, burn, pool, slice, holder, box. Put the literal word next to every joke.
- The dollar is the star, and it is the box's coin pile. Never call a person a dollar, and never make fun of anyone
  for buying, selling or taking a slice.
- The token is 1. It is not a dollar and not a stable coin. Never write that the token equals, follows or stands for a dollar.
- Every post says what the token is, with the words "The token is 1." or "the token 1".
- Write the ticker as 1, with no dollar sign, and say "the token 1" when a bare 1 could read as a number. Never
  put a number right before it. Say "tokens" when you count them.
- No urgency, no countdowns, no prices and no talk about what the token or a slice might fetch. Never suggest that holding
  longer pays off.
- In pictures, USDC is a plain silver coin with no name, and the dollar in the box is the green cartoon bill, framed
  under glass. The art letters the ticker 1 in rose, the token's color, and never in the dollar's green. In text, name USDC
  where a sentence needs a name.
- Art lettering is in capitals. Everywhere else we write in sentence case. The ticker is 1 everywhere, never with a
  dollar sign.

## Typography

Live text uses three free font families under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, all on Google Fonts.

| Use | Family and weights | Fallback stack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Titles | Bodoni Moda, 500 to 800 | `"Bodoni Moda", "Playfair Display", "Didot", "Bodoni 72", Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif` |
| Body and buttons | Instrument Sans, 400 to 700 | `"Instrument Sans", system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", Roboto, sans-serif` |
| Numbers, amounts, counts | IBM Plex Mono, 400 to 600 | `"IBM Plex Mono", ui-monospace, "SF Mono", Menlo, Consolas, monospace` |

Bodoni Moda has the same thick stems and hairline serifs as the salon capitals in the art. Its hairlines fade at small
sizes, so it stays in titles of 24 px and up and never sets body text. Instrument Sans is plain and easy to read at
small sizes. IBM Plex Mono keeps every digit the same width, so the dollar, a slice and the supply line up.

The pictures need no font at all. Every letter in the art is drawn from the kit's own salon alphabet, described in
[art-notes.md](art-notes.md). The alphabet has no dollar sign, so no picture can letter one. This kit ships no font
files. A site that uses these fonts self-hosts pinned files with their license notice. Body text is at least 16 px
with a line height of 1.5.

## Logo

The mark is the exhibit. A green cartoon one-dollar bill with a big 1 lies on dark red velvet in a deep brass frame
under glass. The frame hangs from a brass hook on the claret wall, with a spotlight's glow behind it, and streaks of
light and a glint shine on the glass. The files are `art/logo.svg` and `art/logo.png` (1024), `art/x-avatar.png`
(400) and `art/logo-48.png`, which shows the mark still reads at 48 px.

- **Construction.** The square is 32 modules wide. One module is 32 px on the 1024 px logo. The bill is 18.4 modules
  wide and 8.36 tall, with its middle 16 modules from the left and 17.4 from the top. Around it, the velvet mat is
  0.05 of the bill's width, the box's depth 0.022 and the brass moulding 0.062, so the frame is 23.33 modules wide
  and 13.29 tall. The hook sits 6.76 modules from the top. The logo's frame is slimmer than the scenes' frames, so
  the bill and its 1 stay big at 48 px.
- **Clear space.** Keep at least 2 modules of bare ground on every side. The square files already include it, and
  `check.mjs` proves it by comparing the logo with its bare ground, pixel by pixel.
- **Round crop.** In a round avatar, the whole mark sits inside 95% of the circle. `check.mjs` proves that the same
  way.
- **Smallest size.** 48 px. Below that, write the name in live text instead.
- **Keep** the claret square, the frame and the bill together, and keep the bill green with its big 1.
- **Don't** recolor the bill (never gold, silver or blue), put the ticker, a dollar sign or other words on the bill,
  give it a face, mirror, stretch, rotate or outline the mark, or set it on a light ground without its claret square.

On the X banner, the bottom-left corner (x 0 to 420, y 270 to 500) is bare ground, and the frame leaves that corner
out, so the avatar can overlap it. `check.mjs` compares that corner with the bare ground pixel by pixel. Keep words at
least 60 px from every edge of the X banner and 48 px from every edge of the other pieces. `check.mjs` measures every
word it draws. The wide box banner can be cropped on narrow screens, so the framed dollar sits in its middle and the
page also shows the name in live text.

## Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Draw the dollar as the green cartoon bill with its big 1, framed under glass | Draw a real note, any real coin's face or any stable coin's logo |
| Draw the token as rose tokens with a rosette, and letter the ticker 1 in rose | Put a 1 on a token, so it reads as a one-dollar coin |
| Draw every letter from the salon alphabet | Copy a known artwork, museum or exhibit, or tape anything to a wall |
| Show a burn as the fire in the marble fireplace, and waiting fees in the jar | Show prices, charts, rockets, arrows that climb, or a dollar that gets bigger |
| Put each fee picture next to its caption | Say the dollar holds up the price of the token or protects it |
| Put the literal line next to the cover line, and say what the token is in every post | Write the ticker with a dollar sign, or put a number right before it |
| Say plainly that a slice is small | Call the token a dollar, a stable coin or a dollar token |

All of these pass WCAG AA for normal text. Night on claret is 1.27:1 and the velvet rope on claret 2.29:1, so they stay
decoration and never mark a control. Silver, rose, teal, flame, ember, brass and green on ivory are all below 3:1, so

- Every SVG carries a title and a description. The storybook uses the same words as alt text.
- Repeat any words from a banner or the cover as live text on the page.
- Never show meaning by color alone. USDC is a plain silver coin and the dollar is a bill. The token has its rosette, a burn
  is a fire, the pool is a fountain, waiting fees are a jar, and the words say it too.
- The art is still. If a site makes the fire flicker or the glass glint, keep it slow, never flash more than three
  times a second, and turn it off for people who ask for reduced motion.
- A screen reader may read a bare 1 as a number. Pages that show the ticker give it an accessible name, "the token 1".
- The dollar, a slice quote, the least amount and the contract address are selectable text, never only pictures.
  Controls are at least 44 by 44 px with a visible focus ring in ivory.

## Homage and IP

- The idea is a satire on tokens that tell a big story about what stands behind them. Here everything in the box is
  on display, and it is one dollar. The homage is in the idea only.
- The name follows a common phrase pattern. The kit names no song, paper or film that uses it.
- Museums, frames, velvet ropes and a shop's framed first dollar are old, common sights. The kit names no museum,
  artwork or artist, copies no known exhibit, and tapes nothing to a wall.
- All art here is original and drawn in code for this kit. Nothing is traced, downloaded or generated from other
  images, and the pictures use no font.
- No real brands, logos, products, people, mascots or characters.

<!-- bill-rules:start -->
**The bill.** For this kit only, the version 1 ban on a banknote is lifted, within these rules.

- The bill is our own design, one side only, and clearly a cartoon. It does not follow the real note's layout.
- No real portrait. The middle of the bill is a round medallion of fine lines with a big 1.
- No Treasury or Federal Reserve seal, no serial numbers and no real signatures.
- No "Federal Reserve Note" or "The United States of America" text, and no other words of a real note.
- Green ink, an ornate border and a big ONE or 1 are fine. The bill's only words are 1 and ONE.
- No dollar sign appears anywhere: not on the bill, not as a price and not before the ticker.

`check.mjs` refuses any of these words in the docs outside this block, in every SVG title and description and in
every word a picture letters, and it allows the mark to letter nothing but the bill's 1 and ONE.
<!-- bill-rules:end -->

- The rule that the token is not a dollar stays, and it is the joke. The token never equals or follows a dollar, and
  no line says it does.
- USDC is written in plain text only where a sentence or a setting needs it. It never appears in a picture.
- Square Hole is named as the platform. This kit names no other Square Hole token or collection.
- The ticker 1 is short, and other tokens may use 1 too. Live pages always show the chain and the full contract
  address.
- "One Dollar" names a real currency, the box is priced in a dollar stable coin, and the art shows a cartoon bill.
  Counsel still has to look at the name and the ticker, the bill, the pile and its slice rule, and the license for
  publishing this kit. See [details.md](details.md).
