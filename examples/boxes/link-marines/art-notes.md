# How the Link Marines art is built

Every picture in `art/` is generated by the code in `src/`. `build-art.mjs` writes each SVG and renders its PNG
with `@resvg/resvg-js` 2.6.2, with system fonts switched off. Change the source, rebuild, and run `check.mjs`.
Never edit an SVG or PNG by hand, because the check compares them with a fresh build.

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `src/palette.mjs` | The ten brand colors and the fixed shades used inside the art. No other file defines a color |
| `src/doc.mjs` | The small SVG builder: shared definitions, shapes, paths for circles and rounded rectangles, placing and number rounding |
| `src/render.mjs` | Finds `@resvg/resvg-js` and turns an SVG into PNG bytes |
| `src/type.mjs` | The stencil alphabet and the lettering that draws it, with a record of where every word lands |
| `src/pen.mjs` | The pen: filled shapes with an outline, thick strokes with an ink edge, halftone dots and clipping |
| `src/poster.mjs` | The ground of every picture: the mess hall's tiled wall in five inks |
| `src/face.mjs` | Faces: the head, ears, neck, eyes, brows, nose, mouths with coins, mustache, stubble, face paint, freckles, a smudge and braids |
| `src/gear.mjs` | Headgear: the steel helmet with its options, patrol cap, cook's cap, flight cap, diving helmet, fur cap, peaked cap, beanie and bush hat |
| `src/tools.mjs` | Things a marine holds: a sleeve and fist, spoon, ladle, bugle, cup, fork, wrench and field glasses |
| `src/marine.mjs` | One marine, head and shoulders, built from a spec: shirt, face, hat, napkin, braids and what the hands hold |
| `src/coins.mjs` | The plain gold coin, the bitten coin and the orange dog tag |
| `src/items.mjs` | The 12 marine variants |
| `src/props.mjs` | Story symbols: floor, mess table, coin heap, mess tin, pool, crates, peeking helmets, bell, padlock, crossed-out key, footlocker, raise bar, arrows, signs, button and pointer, frame, tray and jar |
| `src/scenes.mjs` | The 7 story scenes, with their captions and alt text |
| `src/brand.mjs` | Logo, X avatar, X banner, cover and box banner, and the bare walls the check compares them with |

## The look

A field mess hall, drawn like a screen-printed poster. Every shape is a flat ink with a boot-black outline. Shade is
always halftone dots printed in a second ink, never a gradient, and there are no glows or textures beyond the dots,
the tile grout, stitches and wood grain. The marines are caricatures with big round noses, blushing cheeks and small
eyes, and every one of them has a gold coin to eat.

- **Outline.** Each piece of art has one outline width: 10 px on the 1024 px marine pictures, 7 px in the scenes,
  8 px on the cover, 6 px on the X banner, 5 to 6 px on the box banner, and 0.62 modules on the mark's patch. Small
  things in a scene use 4 to 6 px.
- **Halftone.** Dots on a staggered grid, 13 or 14 px apart in most places, each dot 0.24 of that spacing in radius.
  Dots go on the right side of every shape and in folds. A light flat shape goes on the left.
- **No blue.** The palette has no blue or cyan at all, and the check refuses any. The diver's glass and the goggles
  are sage, and the night is a dark olive.

## The wall

- **Tiles.** Square tiles in one ink, with grout lines in a second, slightly darker ink, `tile / 16` px wide. The
  tiles are 128 px on the marine pictures and the mark, 100 px in the scenes and on the banners, and 105 px on the
  cover. The grid is centred on the picture, so the middle of every picture falls on a grout line.
- **Five walls.** Olive, orange, khaki and night for the marine pictures, paper for the scenes. The brand pieces use
  olive.
- **Nothing radiates.** The wall has no rays, discs or bursts, so no picture puts a soldier in front of anything that
  looks like a flag.
- **Floor and table.** The scenes have a crate-wood floor from y 1040, and most have the long mess table. A marine
  sits behind the table, so the table hides where the bust ends.

## The stencil alphabet

- Letters sit on a grid 10 units tall. Each glyph is one or more square-ended strokes 2.3 units wide, with thin
  bridges 0.7 units wide cut through them, like letters sprayed through a crate stencil.
- Letters sit 1.7 units apart and lines 3.4 units apart. Most letters are 6 to 7.6 units wide, M is 8.8, W is 10.4,
  and I is a single stem.
- An optional second print, the same stencil moved down and right, gives titles their orange shadow. It moves 0.9
  units by default and 1.1 units under the box's name. Its bridges reach back to the first print's bridges, so every
  bridge shows the wall behind it.
- The set is the 26 capitals, the digits 0 to 9, `. , ! ? ' % + - :` and the space.
- `lettering()` never writes the name of the project behind the price coin. It writes LINK only inside the box's own
  name, so no picture labels a coin.
- Every block of words records its outside box, so `check.mjs` can keep words 48 px from the edges, and 60 px on the
  X banner.
- To add a glyph, add `{ w, d, cuts }` to the table in `src/type.mjs`: its width in units, its strokes as path data
  with 0 at the cap line and 10 on the baseline, and its bridges as rectangles.

## The marines

A marine is a bust in a box 1000 units wide and 1000 tall, with the face centred on x 500. `marine()` places that box
anywhere at any size and keeps the outline the same width in the picture.

- **Face.** A spec gives the head's width and height, the jaw and the chin. The brow line sits 38% of the way down the
  head, and the eyes, nose and mouth hang from it. Every face is ration paper with blush on the cheeks.
- **Eyes.** Hidden under a brim, dots, wide, happy, sleepy, squinting or winking.
- **Mouths.** A chomp holds a coin in the teeth, whole or bitten. A shout shows a coin on the tongue. There are also a
  grin, a smile, a chew, an O and no mouth for the bugler.
- **Extras.** Puffed cheeks, stubble, face paint, freckles, an oil smudge, a mustache, one or two braids, a napkin and
  field glasses.
- **Hats.** Each hat gives a back part drawn before the head and a front part drawn after the face. None carries an
  emblem, a rank or a badge. The officer's cap has a plain round button.
- **Hands.** A sleeve rises from below the picture to a fist that holds a tool. The spoon and the fork can be drawn
  empty, as in the waiting scene.

## Scenes and brand pieces

- **Scenes** are 1200 by 1200, on the paper wall with 100 px tiles and the floor at y 1040. Captions and alt text live
  next to each scene in `src/scenes.mjs`, and `check.mjs` makes sure `storybook.md` uses the same words. The two
  marines who wait and eat at the table are variants 01 and 05, the framed marine is 09 and the marine with the empty
  tray is 11.
- **The mark** is built on a module of 1/32 of its square. The khaki patch has a radius of 13 modules, and its stitches
  run 1.1 modules inside the rim. Variant 01 is scaled to 0.89 of the bust box and cut off by the patch's circle.
  `check.mjs` renders the logo and the avatar next to their bare walls and proves that 2 modules of clear space stay
  empty and that the patch sits inside 95% of a round crop.
- **The X banner** keeps x 0 to 420, y 270 to 500 as bare wall for the avatar, with the name and the cover line above
  it and two marines at the table on the right. `check.mjs` proves the corner pixel by pixel.
- **The cover** puts the name in two lines and the cover line on the left, and variant 01 at the table on the right.
- **The box banner** seats all twelve marines side by side at one long table, in variant order, under the name.

## Rules for new art

- Start every picture with `ground()` from `src/poster.mjs`, and keep the wall free of rays, discs and bursts.
- Use only the palette. A new shade goes into `src/palette.mjs`, and `check.mjs` refuses colors from anywhere else and
  any blue.
- Draw every letter from the stencil alphabet. Keep words in pictures short, never label a coin, and never write the
  name of the project behind the price coin.
- Draw every word with `lettering` in `src/type.mjs`, so `--no-words` can leave it out.
- One marine per item picture, head and shoulders, in the same bust box, so the collection reads as one set.
- Keep every face ration paper. Tell marines apart by hats, hair, faces and what they hold.
- No weapons, no real emblems, insignia, ranks, flags, unit patches or camouflage, no real people, no mascots or known
  characters, no coin logos, hexagons or chains.
- To add a variant, add an entry to `ART` in `src/items.mjs` and its name to `VARIANT_NAMES` in `assign.mjs`, in the
  same order. Then rebuild, regenerate `assignment.json` and run the check.
- The assignment depends on how many variants there are. Changing the count changes the picture of almost every
  marine, and only a count that divides 420 keeps every picture equally common and every squad complete. Fix the set
  before the collection is created.

## Rebuild

```sh
node examples/boxes/link-marines/build-art.mjs
node examples/boxes/link-marines/assign.mjs > examples/boxes/link-marines/assignment.json
node examples/boxes/link-marines/check.mjs
```

The build needs no network and no fonts. `src/render.mjs` first looks for `@resvg/resvg-js` as a normal
dependency, then in the workspace package `packages/cards`, which pins it. If the kit is copied out of this
repository, install `@resvg/resvg-js` 2.6.2 next to it. Another renderer version may draw slightly different
pixels, and the check will say so.

## Word-free references

`node examples/boxes/link-marines/build-art.mjs --no-words` writes only `art/reference/`: the cover, the box banner and
the seven scenes as PNGs with no lettering, as reference pictures of the kit's world. While it runs, `withoutWords` in
`src/type.mjs` makes `lettering` draw nothing, add no stencil mask and put nothing in `DRAWN`, and `measure` still sizes
every block. The crates, the footlocker, the signs, the button and the mess tin stay where they are, blank. The name,
the cover line, the 420 on the crate, the numbers, CHOMP! and the labels go; the tiled wall, the marines, the table,
the coins, the tags and the other props stay. The normal build does not change.
