# Paper Certificates brand book

## Identity

**Name:** Gold Certificates, then Paper Certificates. **Ticker:** SCRIP. **Price coin:** the gold coin, a token backed
by gold, for Gold Certificates; the index coin, a token that follows a fund of a big stock index, for Paper
Certificates. **In the box:** 1,929 joke share certificates, sealed until drawn, and 12 of them are blue chips.

The two editions are one idea priced in two coins. Gold Certificates launches first. Paper Certificates follows when
the index coin's pool is deep enough. Each edition is its own box with its own price coin, and everything in this book
holds for both unless it names one.

**One-sentence joke:** Old gold certificates turned paper into gold, and this box turns gold back into paper.

**Cover line:** "Gold in, paper out."

**The literal line next to it:** "In plain words, after graduation the buyers' share of every fee buys SCRIP and burns it, and the sellers' share buys SCRIP for the certificates."

**The paper edition's cover line:** "Stock in, paper out." Its joke is that shares went from paper to tokens, and this
box turns the tokens back into paper. The literal line is the same.

**Story hook:** In an old transfer office stands an iron safe marked 1,929. Inside are 1,929 sealed envelopes, and each
holds a share certificate of a company that never existed. Every buy and every sale pays a small fee in the gold
coin. The buyers' coins buy SCRIP, the box's own token, and that SCRIP goes into the stove. The sellers' coins buy
SCRIP too, and it is shared by all the certificates. A certificate out of the safe keeps its part. The part of a
certificate still sealed in the safe burns. The gold coin is never put in the stove.

The joke is on old paper finance and on the box itself, which swaps real coins for paper. It is never on the people who
buy, sell or hold, and never on the people behind the gold coin or the index coin.

## Voice and tone

We sound like an old clerk at a transfer office counter who explains a certificate to a customer, precise, patient and
a little dry. Pictures and numbers come first, words second. We say what happens in the order it happens. When
something can go wrong, we say it plainly and leave the joke out.

- Use the plain words: box, fee, coin, pool, burn, holder, certificate, draw. Put the literal word next to every joke.
- "Burned" is said only of SCRIP. The gold coin and the index coin are swapped, held or paid, and never burned.
- A coupon is the picture of SCRIP, a small lilac slip. When a page says coupon, it says SCRIP next to it.
- No urgency, no countdowns, no prices, and no talk about what SCRIP, a certificate or a price coin might fetch.
- In pictures the price coin has no letters. In kit texts it is "the gold coin" or "the index coin". A live page names
  it by the ticker its listing shows.
- A blue chip is part of a small special series. We never say it is better, and we never tell people which
  certificate they will draw.
- Engraved lettering is in capitals. Everywhere else we write in sentence case.

Words and shapes we never use: earn, yield, returns, gains, profit, income, passive, investment, dividend, interest,
reward, prize, jackpot, wealth, upside, moon, 100x, APY, ROI, guaranteed, "get in early", "floor price", pump, unlock,
seamless, revolutionary, journey, hodl, "diamond hands", bullish. For the certificates: rare, rarity, grail, valuable,
worth, lottery, gamble, loot, win, lucky. For the price coins: eat, eaten, and any sentence that says the gold coin, a
stock or the index coin is burned. No dash asides, no emoji decoration, and no prices, dates or wallet addresses in
posts or pictures. No claim that a burn sets a price or helps anyone who holds SCRIP, the gold coin or the index coin.

Some names never appear at all. They are the gold coin's own name, ticker and issuer, the index coin's own name, ticker
and issuer, the index and the fund the index coin follows, real stock exchanges and real companies. `check.mjs` holds
SHA-256 fingerprints of these names and refuses them in every doc, every SVG title and description, every lettered word
and every source file, so the kit never spells them, not even in its own check. The avoid list above is the only place the words in it may appear.

and a little gold foil. Every shape is a flat color in an ink outline, and tone comes from lines, never from a gradient.

In the story, gold coins stand for the gold coin and steel coins with a basket for the index coin. Lilac coupons stand
for SCRIP. The iron safe marked 1,929 is the box, the balance scale is the box's own pool, and the stove burns SCRIP.
They are plain symbols. When a page shows real balances, it also writes each coin's name in text.

## Typography

Live text uses three free font families under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, all on Google Fonts.

| Use | Family and weights | Fallback stack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Titles | Old Standard TT, 400 and 700 | `"Old Standard TT", "Bodoni 72", Didot, Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif` |
| Body and buttons | Libre Franklin, 400 to 700 | `"Libre Franklin", "Franklin Gothic Book", system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", Roboto, sans-serif` |
| Numbers, counts, ids | Cutive Mono, 400 | `"Cutive Mono", "Courier New", ui-monospace, "SF Mono", Menlo, monospace` |

Old Standard TT follows the Roman type of old printed documents, close to the kit's engraved letters. Libre Franklin is
a plain sans with the look of old stock tables and stays easy to read at small sizes. Cutive Mono looks like a clerk's
typewriter and keeps every digit the same width, so certificate numbers and counts line up.

The pictures need no font at all. Every letter in the art is drawn by the kit's own engraver's alphabet, described in
[art-notes.md](art-notes.md). This kit ships no font files. A site that uses these fonts self-hosts pinned files with
their license notice. Body text is at least 16 px with a line height of 1.5.

## Logo

The mark is a gold foil seal, the kind pressed onto an old share certificate. It has a scalloped edge, a dark ring of
white lacework, a gold middle with a fine rosette, and an engraved S for SCRIP. It is the same for both editions. The
files are `art/logo.svg` and `art/logo.png` (1024), `art/x-avatar.png` (400) and `art/logo-48.png`, which shows the
mark still reads at 48 px.

- **Construction.** One module is 1/32 of the square, 32 px on the 1024 px logo. The seal is centred and reaches 13.4
  modules from its centre at the tips of its 44 scallops. The dark ring runs from 8.9 to 11.2 modules, the rosette
  fills the gold middle, and the S is 9.1 modules tall with a gold-brown shadow set down and right. The outline is a
  third of a module.
- **Clear space.** Keep at least 2 modules free on every side. The square files already include it, and `check.mjs`
  proves that the border holds only bare paper.
- **Round crop.** In a round avatar, the whole seal sits inside 95% of the circle. `check.mjs` proves this too.
- **Smallest size.** 48 px. Below that, write SCRIP in live text instead.
- **Keep** the scallops, the dark ring and the S together.
- **Don't** recolor the seal, swap the S for another letter or the ticker, add words around the ring, add ribbons,
  stretch, rotate or outline the mark, or set it on a dark ground without its paper square.

On each X banner, the bottom-left corner (x 0 to 420, y 270 to 500) holds only bare paper, so the avatar can overlap
it. `check.mjs` renders both banners and their bare grounds and compares that corner pixel by pixel. Keep words at least
60 px from every edge of the banners and 48 px from the edges of every other piece. The wide box banner can be cropped
on narrow screens, so the page also shows the name in live text.

## The certificates

Every certificate is one sheet, with a share certificate above a perforation and a sheet of five coupons below it. The
certificate has a guilloche frame with four corner rosettes, a number box, a box that says ONE OF 1,929, a vignette in
an oval, the company's name, the line THIS CERTIFIES NOTHING, a seal and two signatures. Each company is printed in its
own ink on a mat of a deeper shade. The blue chips are printed in blue, with a gold seal and a gold ribbon that says
BLUE CHIP.

THIS CERTIFIES NOTHING is the joke and the disclaimer at once. It stays on every certificate. The twelve pictures and
their counts are in the [README](README.md), and each real certificate prints its own item id in its number box.

## The hidden picture

Until a certificate is drawn, it and every ticket show one picture, a cream envelope closed with a red wax seal that
shows a question mark. A box with hidden art keeps this picture on chain, where it must be one line of printable ASCII
of at most 4,096 bytes. `art/hidden.svg` is that line, and `check.mjs` holds it to the limit. `art/hidden.png` is the
same picture rendered for previews. Today's create page fills the field with its own default picture. When the page
takes a custom picture, this file goes in as it is.

## Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Invent every company, as a pun on a trading word, with a trade that fits it | Use a real company, brand, logo, product or stock exchange, or a name that sounds like one |
| Draw certificates as invented share certificates with THIS CERTIFIES NOTHING on them | Copy a real certificate, a banknote, a coin, a stamp or a real portrait |
| Show the gold coin as a gold disc with a small bar, and the index coin as a steel disc with a basket | Draw a coin's real logo, a face, a letter or a symbol on any coin |
| Show SCRIP as a lilac coupon with a violet rosette | Write the ticker or a number on a coupon |
| Say SCRIP is burned, and that a burn sets no price | Put the gold coin, a stock or the index coin in the fire, in words or in a picture |
| Call a blue chip part of a special series, with the same part of the fees | Call it better, or hint at which certificate a draw gives |
| Say a certificate is a picture, no share and no claim on gold, a stock or a fund | Suggest a certificate stands for a real share, or can be traded in for gold |
| Draw every letter with the engraver's alphabet | Use a font in a picture |
| Put each fee picture next to its caption | Show price charts, rockets or anything rising in value |
| Keep the joke on old paper finance and on the box | Make fun of the people who hold the gold coin, the index coin or SCRIP |

The first fifteen pass WCAG AA for normal text, and ink on paper passes AAA. Ink on wood passes for large text only,
so on a page it carries headings and labels. Gold on paper is decoration only, and gold lettering on paper always uses
gold ink. `check.mjs` recomputes every ratio in this table.

- Every SVG carries a title and a description. The storybook uses the same words as alt text.
- Repeat any words from a banner, the cover or a certificate as live text on the page.
- Never show burned, waiting, sealed or blue chip by color alone. Say it in words.
- The art is still. If a site animates the fire or a coupon, keep it gentle and turn it off for people who ask for
  reduced motion.
- The fine lines of the engraving are decoration. Never put small live text on top of them.
- Amounts, certificate ids and the contract address are selectable text, never only pictures. Controls are at least
  44 by 44 px with a visible focus ring, ink on light grounds and paper on iron.

## Homage and IP

- The box is a nod to old share certificates and to the people who collect them, by idea only. Its certificates, seals,
  frames and vignettes are drawn for this kit. No real certificate, banknote, coin or stamp was traced or copied, and
  none is drawn.
- The companies are invented puns on trading words. No real company, brand or product is named or drawn. The names
  have not been checked against registered company names and marks yet.
- The gold coin and the index coin are never named in the kit, and their issuers, the index and the fund behind it
  never appear. Every public page carries the "not affiliated" and "not a share" lines from the voice table.
- Old paper money once carried the name gold certificate. This box borrows the idea of paper and gold for its joke. It
  draws no banknote and makes no promise to exchange anything for gold.
- Guilloche bands, rosettes, seals and the engraver's Roman letters are old printing crafts, drawn here in code. None
  copies the work of a real security printer.
- All art here is original and drawn in code for this kit. Nothing is traced, downloaded or generated from other
  images, and the pictures use no font.
- Square Hole is named as the platform. This kit names no other Square Hole token or collection.
- SCRIP may be used by other tokens. Live pages always show the chain and the full contract address.
- Counsel still has to look at the names, the "not a share" line, the word coupon, the paid draws, the certificates'
  part of the fees, the price coins and the license for publishing this kit. See [details.md](details.md).
